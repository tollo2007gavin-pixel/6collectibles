# Experimental four-corner inspection

Reviews now include zoomable corner marking tools and portable human-label exports. See [Mark cards and compare improvements](PIXEL_LABELING.md) for the separate development-only tuning and frozen-validation GitHub workflow. The active detector is not automatically modified by these reports.

## Selective edge classification

The GitHub strategy `edge_uncertainty` only marks candidate light spots; it does not run the whitening classifier. Select `edge_classifier` for the three-way classifications below. Reports explicitly name their analysis mode. Classifier reviews have a separate expandable orange overlay for inconclusive spots, including small possible chips. Those spots are not certified clean and are not discarded from review.

`--classify-whitening --edge-filter` adds `edge-evidence-v2` classifications to all original candidates, including previously excluded T/G spots. Printed-art/background exclusions remain likely artifacts. The original evidence path requires card association, normalized core area >=40, PCA axis ratio <=3, fill >=0.25, brightness change >=max(20,4*local noise), and saturation loss >=15 or stronger brightness >=max(32,5*noise). Strong patches touching the uncertain edge become likely whitening. A strong interior patch may also qualify if its box is within 2.4% of the crop width from a supported edge patch in the same corner. This is one hop only; it cannot chain across the card. No large-spot exception exists.

V2 adds a second evidence path for formerly inconclusive spots. `patch_evidence.py` measures connected half-peak light patches at two normalized Gaussian scales (8 and 12 pixels at a 500-pixel tile, against a 1-pixel smoothed image). The seed may lie within four normalized pixels of the original threshold core, matching the extraction neighborhood radius. A refined peak cannot jump to a detached patch. Both scales must be unclipped, compact (axis ratio <=3, fill >=0.35), 40–2500 normalized pixels in area, within a 2.5-fold area ratio, and at least 25% in the uncertain edge. The broader patch contrast must be >=max(20,4*local residual noise). Attachment requires either patch proximity within three normalized pixels of the estimated card, or a core within three pixels of that edge linked to the patch within four pixels and saturation loss >=15. These are bounded evidence regions, not measurements of physical chip area. Thresholds, patch boxes, attachment distances, and reasons are saved. A/B exclusions and unreliable edges still cannot be rescued by this path.

Classifier mode alone also retries failed outlines using hue measured in the inward card quadrant. It requires enough blue samples and concentrated hue, then repeats all three saturation silhouettes, anchoring, size and agreement checks. Existing agreeing estimates are unchanged; ambiguous retries remain unconfirmed. This addresses blue/purple background contamination without changing candidate-only modes or the centering pipeline.

V2 was developed against four additional user-confirmed misses in the November 16 run: cards 10 TL, 11 BR, 13 BL and 14 BL. Recovering these examples is development evidence, not independent accuracy. Previously unseen compact glare may still pass. The preceding complete version is commit `fe90cdd`; no new API calls are used by this classifier.

Spots outside all raw card estimates by >=1% of crop width and not covered by bright-edge uncertainty become likely artifacts. Remaining weak, tiny, elongated or poorly located spots are inconclusive, never clean. Long damage and compact glare are possible; these heuristics can abstain incorrectly or produce false positives. Candidate IDs only identify output records/supporting neighbors; image IDs and human labels are never features. Thresholds and reasons are saved with results.

The classifier was developed with card 5 labels and checked against known card 4 examples. That is development/regression evidence, not held-out accuracy. Card 5 remained consistent under mild exposure/blur perturbations and ±10% individual threshold changes. Card 4 deteriorated under 10% darkening; no general accuracy percentage is established. Keep this strategy experimental. Fallback tag: whitening-before-classifier-v1 (4801b3d); legacy and edge_uncertainty still work.

## Printed artwork exclusion

Clipped strips have a separate rule: minimum visible short side 3% and long side 20% of the crop, at least 85% rectangular occupancy, and placement within 0.6% of both inward crop sides. Clipped strips use a 0.5% area floor; ordinary rectangles retain 0.8%. Both retain the 40% ceiling and edge-uncertainty protection. This handles a narrow portion of artwork cut by the crop without lowering the ordinary rectangle minimum for all spots. The former shared area floor incorrectly rejected a qualifying 109-by-16-pixel artwork strip in the November 16 batch.

`--art-filter` detects pale, roughly rectangular regions extending through both inward crop sides. It requires reliable edge estimates, a local blue reference, minimum width/height (6% of crop), area (0.8%–40% of crop) and rectangular occupancy (60%). A 2%-of-crop allowance captures blurred printed-art boundaries. The mask is restricted to estimated card interior outside the edge uncertainty zone, and only wholly contained candidates are excluded. Exclusions retain their IDs and receive A labels. This is a specific normalized-corner layout heuristic, not semantic artwork recognition; ambiguous/unsupported cases remain eligible. It does not classify all white interior pixels as artwork or restore a large-spot exception.

## Optional edge uncertainty strategy

Edge diagnostics now distinguish overlap with any estimated card interior (`card_associated`) from spots outside every estimate but still inside the safety margin (`off_card_uncertain`). This is location evidence only; it does not override candidate retention or certify damage. Raw outside distances and overlap fractions are recorded. `card5_edge_audit.py` produces a one-card comparison from the saved review and a source-hash-bound human-label JSON file; labels never enter the detector.

`--edge-filter` adds a conservative background gate before grain and geometry. `edge_uncertainty.py` creates three blue silhouettes using saturation factors .55/.65/.75, rejects implausible or strongly disagreeing estimates, and protects the union with a 4%-of-tile margin. A connected candidate is excluded only if all its pixels are outside the protected region. Bright neutral components touching the edge are treated as uncertain. This deliberately retains more candidates against white/glared backgrounds. It does not claim exact outlines, calibrated uncertainty, or reliable dust/damage separation. The grain/triangle stages remain unchanged and may still reject a genuine chip.

`B` means background rejection; `G` and geometry letters retain their prior meanings. An unconfirmed edge skips the B filter. Original pixels remain unchanged; amber shading exists only in the audit overlay. Saved candidate IDs and source hashes make baseline comparisons possible. No large-spot exemption has been reintroduced.

Offline evaluation tools: `evaluate_edge_filter.py` measures margin sensitivity and known-example perturbations; `verify_edge_replay.py` checks exact legacy decisions and repeated enhanced decisions against saved JSON. These are regression checks, not independent accuracy evaluations. The saved fallback tag is pixel-fallback-before-edge-uncertainty.

Optional grain filtering (`--grain-filter`) runs before geometry, preserving original candidate IDs and recording G exclusions. It estimates local brightness-residual noise using median absolute deviation in nearby blue pixels within the estimated card interior, excluding each candidate and a small margin. This is not a full validated segmentation of the area between the outer card edge and the interior artwork rectangle. Border/artwork contamination remains possible.

The conservative initial rule requires at least 100 reference pixels, noise sigma between 4 and 18, candidate area below 40 normalized pixels, 75th-percentile brightness residual below a noise-dependent threshold (13–30), and 75th-percentile saturation loss below 30. Large or strongly contrasting marks survive. Insufficient or unstable reference skips grain rejection; overall output remains needs_review. Glare classification and damage accuracy are not established by this rule. Compare G labels against human-reviewed chips before increasing its strength.

Run `python pixel_review.py "reviewed.html" --out "new-output-folder"` to inspect the saved Corner close-ups images in a portable reviewed HTML. The output folder must be new. Outputs include an embedded comparison gallery, annotated sheets, and JSON measurements. It uses no model calls.

The input is the exact existing 2 by 2 corner-tip sheet made by `card_utils.corner_closeup_sheet`. Its four tiles are read without resizing, warping, or running the card detector. Captions and separators are excluded. Source hashes and per-corner coordinates are recorded. A card with no saved corner sheet is explicitly skipped. Unknown layouts raise an error.

Red boxes mark local clusters brighter than their neighborhood near blue pixels. These are light-spot candidates, not confirmed chips. All decisions remain `needs_review`. Glare, dust, and textured backgrounds can still trigger candidates; refining those distinctions is deferred. There is no whole-edge inspection or fold classifier. Counts refer to enlarged sheet pixels rather than independent source-photo pixels. Back colors other than blue require separate calibration.

Each corner independently supplies a blue-ink interior mask, using relative saturation to reduce sleeve contamination, morphological opening and a convex hull. The inward crop corner is included to prevent printed artwork from making an internal boundary. Cyan shows only the inner cutoff, 2.4% of tile width inward (12 pixels at a 500-pixel tile). There is no outer cutoff within the saved crop. This is a provisional width, not a learned damage standard. Crop-frame edges are ignored using replicated padding. This segmentation remains approximate and can fail on glare, shadows or blue backgrounds.

The outline mode requires at least half the candidate pixels outward of the inner cutoff. Geometry modes exclude candidates that meet their blue threshold regardless of area. There is no large-spot exception and no L label. Grain filtering remains limited to small weak spots; larger spots still undergo the full geometry test.

Human corrections are shown alongside measurements, never used as detector inputs. No detection accuracy should be inferred from candidate counts. Tests exercise the real sheet layout, caption exclusion, corner attribution, source preservation, repeatability, and rejection of incompatible layouts. Run `python -m unittest test_pixel_whitening`.
